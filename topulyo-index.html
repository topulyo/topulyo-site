Album — Faitiche, Berlin 2026

Quiet Scenes
From Tokyo

Sixteen miniatures for solo cello and Roland 301 Space Echo. The tape used only at the very end — a final pass through mixing. Everything else live.

Extended technique replicating electronic phenomena acoustically: portamento, filter sweeps, granular texture, noise.

Quiet Scenes from Tokyo, but the juxtaposition couldn’t be further from the truth. I lived across from a six-story highway, and the highway symbolized a lot of interpersonal stress I was feeling at the time. I had to turn the highway into the ocean. I had to turn stress into freedom.

Why do I make music?

i make music as a form of documentation. i love every form of it: recording, journaling, photography, video. any thing that shows the era we are alive and is proof of existence. i love collaborating with others and learning from them and sharing. it is a beautiful synergy of collective energy. i always feel like the experiences i share with other artists are so special and deserve to be preserved in time. the background noise is document to the technology. the type of cars passing, the creaking of old wooden floors. it sounds and feels like brooklyn, los angeles, or tokyo. i have an album out called moon set, moon rise which i really wanted to record the room and creaks, the cars passing by. i wanted you to feel the hot summer brooklyn days, the cold winters where your feet are always cold. on my solo album ‘quiet scenes from tokyo’ i wanted to do an escapist album where you feel like you’re in nature in peace. i was going through life’s hardships and rented a small studio across from a 6 story freeway.

What do I want people to leave with after spending a year with me?

after sharing collaborations for a year +, i want people to feel inspired to feel like they can fly on their own. a sense of self empowerment. i want to show that artists are not special they just go through the motions of practice, capturing, editing, producing and releasing. just following basic steps. it is not “hard” but it is the hardest part to do it.

What do I notice that other people don't?

i try to notice what everyone is trying to say. i notice the types of lines people are playing, the volume or space they leave. the form or no form they are trying to create. i feel like team sports is the perfect analogy to jamming. everyone has to find their role whether it be a lead role, someone doing dirty work, offense, defense, getting rebounds. two people can be playing melodies but not all the time. it is important to find your role. bass lines, chordal, supplemental. it is important to know when and how to step up for each position.

Why do I keep journals?

journals to me are a way of printing important ideas. i often hang out with people i admire. i want them to appear in my journal in hopes that someday someone can read that this person was having a tough time with money or love. we are all normal people going through normal hardships. i write everything stream of conscious so bad thoughts come out that need to come out, good ideas are re-inforced. writing an idea down prints it from ephemeral to physical. the more important it is the more i’ll be keen on writing it day after day.

What role does Tokyo play in my work?

tokyo is where i live right now. it offers the highest ceiling for happiness to me. it offers a walkable city, with a language i don’t fully understand. it is fun to learn the language and the language feels like memorizing music and phrases by john coltrane. it doesn’t sound like anything at first, but the more familiar you become it feels like you have a super power to slow down time. recognize words with in phrases and eventually entire phrases. it is beautiful, scary and exciting all at once.

Why the cello?

going back to my sports analogy, i feel like cello is an uncommon position in sports. the cello can be an amazing lead player, it can be supplemental, chordal, bass. i think of it as a ‘draymond green’ of basketball. it can play offense when needed, it can get rebounds, play defense, it can play point guard. it is extremely versatile and often times needs to be the best listener in the group because there is not a conventional position for it to play outside of classical music.

Based in Tokyo. Teaching in person and online.
